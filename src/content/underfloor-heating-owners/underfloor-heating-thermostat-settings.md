**The thermostat is a request for heat, not proof that heat is reaching the floor.** This is especially important with wet underfloor heating, where heat-source controls, pumps and manifold zones sit between a room controller and warmth in the room.

## Five things to identify before changing a setting

| Screen or control | Question to ask the model manual |
| --- | --- |
| Large temperature number | Is it measured room air, desired target or a floor sensor? |
| Smaller target | Is it for the current period, next period or a temporary hold? |
| Flame, wave or heating icon | Does it mean heat requested, an active output or something else? |
| Scheduled, manual, away or holiday mode | Which mode is active, and what will return it to normal? |
| Programme time | Does the room **start asking** for warmth then, or should it **be comfortable by** then? |

Different models use different symbols. Do not copy an online guide for another thermostat simply because the screens look similar.

## What temperature should you set?

First establish **what is being measured**. A room-air target and a floor-probe target are different quantities. There is no single number in this guide that should replace the installed floor limit, your flooring manufacturer's conditions or your thermostat's instructions.

For a normal comfort adjustment, record the existing user setting and change only the documented room target or schedule. If the display is controlling floor temperature, or you cannot tell, identify the sensor mode from your records or ask the installer before making a change. Do not enter installer menus to remove a limit.

### Manufacturer examples that explain confusing behaviour

| Model or feature | What the manufacturer documents | Useful interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Heatmiser neoStat preheat | [Optimum Start and Rate of Change](https://faq.heatmiser.com/hc/en-us/articles/360001725419-Do-the-neoStat-s-learn-the-heat-up-time-in-my-home-Preheat) let the controller learn heating time and begin before a scheduled comfort period | Heating before the displayed programme time can be expected operation; it does not, by itself, prove a stuck control |
| Heatmiser neoStat WiFi hold | Its [product guidance](https://www.heatmiser.com/neostat-wifi/) describes a timed temperature hold that returns to the programme when it expires | A target that later changes back may be an expired hold rather than a lost setting |
| Warmup Tempo with specified floating-floor settings | [Warmup's Tempo explanation](https://www.warmup.co.uk/underfloor-heating/questions/programming/cant-program-tempo-read-air-temperature) describes restrictions on air-sensing selection and higher targets for those settings | A restricted target can be deliberate protection; do not change the recorded floor type to bypass it |

Sources checked 13 November 2026. These examples explain named products, not every thermostat in either brand's range. Use the instructions for your precise model and version.

## Why the heating icon can appear while the room remains cold

A typical room controller observes a temperature below its target and requests heat. The system may still be in a heat-source priority period, delayed by controls, or unable to deliver the required heat. Another zone may be warm while the affected room does not respond.

An active icon therefore does **not** prove that an individual water loop has flow or that an actuator is faulty. The useful question is whether the room temperature actually responds, over a period appropriate to the installed floor construction and control strategy.

If the shortfall persists, record times and actual temperatures before seeking professional help. See [the safe no-warmth checklist](/underfloor-heating-owners/underfloor-heating-not-getting-warm/).

## Room sensors and floor probes are not interchangeable

A room-air sensor tries to represent occupied comfort. A floor probe may instead control surface warmth or protect a flooring finish against excess temperature. Some electric and wet-system thermostats use both.

If a thermostat shows a higher number than the room feels, confirm whether it displays the **floor** rather than the **air**. Do not override a protective limit or change sensor configuration to make the room heat harder. Floor manufacturer conditions and installer settings take precedence.

Also notice the sensor's surroundings: direct sun, a draughty doorway, curtains or nearby appliances can affect readings. Record possible discrepancies using a separate thermometer in a consistent occupied location; have a suitable professional assess persistent sensor problems.

## One normal-user check at a time

1. Photograph or write down the existing mode, target and programme.
2. Find the correct manufacturer manual using the make and model on a safely accessible label.
3. Check a documented normal-user action, such as returning from holiday mode or canceling a temporary hold.
4. Record the time and actual room temperature before and after a suitable observation period.
5. Stop if the control is unfamiliar, protected or behaving unexpectedly; ask the installer rather than experimenting with wiring or calibration.

A controller may begin heating in advance through an adaptive-start feature. Another may change targets only at the specified start time. This difference can explain a cold breakfast without proving a broken heating system.

## Make a thermostat translation record

Copy these fields into your notes before contacting the installer. Photographs of the normal screen and your recorded programme can help; do not remove a controller from the wall to find a hidden label.

| Field | What to record |
| --- | --- |
| Room and system | Room name; wet or electric, if known |
| Make and model | Exact name from existing paperwork or a safely visible label |
| Current display | Number shown, units and any icons |
| Sensor meaning | Air, floor or unknown; note the manual page that identifies it |
| Active mode | Programme, manual, timed hold, away or holiday |
| Desired comfort period | When you need the room comfortable, rather than just when you noticed the icon |
| Observation | Time, actual room reading and what changed after an ordinary user adjustment |

**Illustrative example:** a timed hold expires and the target returns to the next programme level. That sequence is different from the room failing to warm while a higher target remains active. Record which happened so support can investigate the right issue. It is not a reason to raise protected floor limits or adjust the manifold.
