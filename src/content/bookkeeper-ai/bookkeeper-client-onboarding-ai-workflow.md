New-client onboarding contains a lot of repeatable communication.

That makes it a good candidate for structured AI assistance—as long as the AI is helping with the wording and organization rather than inventing the engagement.

The workbook's onboarding prompts cover:

- new-client information requests;
- plain-English engagement summaries;
- software setup instructions;
- onboarding checklists;
- first-meeting agenda emails;
- post-onboarding follow-up.

Those pieces can be connected into one workflow.

## Stage 1: confirm the engagement facts first

Before prompting, identify the facts that already exist.

Depending on the practice, those may include:

- client or business name;
- services agreed;
- start date;
- fee and payment terms;
- accounting software;
- documents required;
- access required;
- response expectations;
- responsibilities of the bookkeeper and the client.

The prompt should use those facts.

It should not decide the scope, fee, legal terms, or professional responsibilities on the practice's behalf.

## Stage 2: draft the information request

A new client often needs to supply several items.

AI can organize a supplied list into a clear email with:

- a short welcome;
- the documents or information required;
- a brief reason for each item where useful;
- a deadline;
- a clear next step.

The list itself should come from the practice's actual onboarding requirements.

Do not let a generic model response become the source of what records the client is legally or professionally required to provide.

## Stage 3: create a plain-English engagement summary

The workbook includes a prompt for summarizing an engagement letter in plain language.

That can be useful for client understanding.

The safe pattern is:

1. start from the signed or approved engagement terms;
2. supply the relevant terms to the prompt;
3. ask for a plain-English summary;
4. clearly state that the summary does not replace the formal engagement letter;
5. compare the summary against the original before sending.

Do not ask AI to draft missing contractual terms and then treat them as agreed.

## Stage 4: prepare software setup instructions

If the client needs to provide access or complete a setup step, AI can turn your approved procedure into clear instructions.

Keep the instructions tied to:

- the software actually used;
- the access level actually required;
- the practice's security process;
- current vendor instructions where the interface or permissions matter.

Do not request passwords, bank credentials, or other sensitive secrets inside an ordinary AI prompt.

Where the client must use a secure portal or approved access method, direct them there.

## Stage 5: build the onboarding checklist

A checklist helps both sides see what remains open.

Useful sections may include:

- engagement complete;
- identity or business details recorded in the proper system;
- accounting software access;
- source documents received;
- payroll information where within scope;
- outstanding invoices or receivables information where relevant;
- opening balances or prior-period records;
- key dates;
- first review meeting;
- unresolved questions.

The exact checklist should reflect the service.

AI can improve the structure; the practice defines the required steps.

## Stage 6: prepare the first-meeting agenda

A short agenda can turn onboarding information into a useful conversation.

Possible agenda items include:

- confirm services and boundaries;
- confirm communication preferences;
- review outstanding information;
- understand the client's reporting needs;
- identify recurring deadlines;
- confirm who will provide which information;
- record questions requiring follow-up.

Ask the model to organize the agenda, not to invent assumptions about the client.

## Stage 7: follow up after the meeting

A post-onboarding message can summarize:

- what was confirmed;
- what remains outstanding;
- who owns each next action;
- any agreed deadline;
- when the client should expect the next update.

This is a good place to use a "facts only" instruction.

If the notes do not contain a date or commitment, the draft should leave it as an unresolved item instead of manufacturing one.

## Protect client information during onboarding

Onboarding often contains more sensitive information than ordinary communication.

Before using AI, apply the [client-data privacy checklist](/bookkeeper-ai/client-data-privacy-for-bookkeeper-ai/).

In particular, avoid casually including:

- government identifiers;
- bank account numbers;
- payment credentials;
- unrelated financial records;
- sensitive personal information;
- confidential documents that the drafting task does not require.

A checklist or email can usually be drafted with placeholders and limited context.

## A reusable onboarding prompt chain

A practical sequence is:

1. **Information request** — organize the approved document/data list.
2. **Engagement summary** — translate supplied approved terms into plain English.
3. **Setup instructions** — explain the practice's actual software-access procedure.
4. **Checklist** — show what is complete and outstanding.
5. **Meeting agenda** — organize the first review conversation.
6. **Follow-up** — confirm verified actions, owners, and deadlines.
7. **Human review and filing** — approve the wording and store the authoritative record in the normal system.

## Keep the client relationship human

Automation should make the routine parts more consistent.

It should not make a new client feel as if the relationship has been handed to a machine.

Review the tone.

Remove generic filler.

Use the client's real context where appropriate and permitted.

Add the professional judgment that explains why a request matters or what the client should expect next.

## Final onboarding check

Before closing the onboarding sequence, verify:

1. Do the services and fees match the approved engagement?
2. Are all requested documents genuinely required for the service?
3. Are access instructions secure and current?
4. Has unnecessary sensitive information been kept out of AI prompts?
5. Are outstanding items clearly identified?
6. Are commitments and deadlines based on actual agreement?
7. Is the final approved record stored in the proper practice system?

The strongest onboarding workflow does not ask AI to run the engagement.

It uses AI to make the repeatable words and structure easier to produce while the bookkeeper keeps control of the facts, relationship, and professional process.
