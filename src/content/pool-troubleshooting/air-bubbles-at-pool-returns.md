A few bubbles immediately after startup may simply be trapped air being purged.

Bubbles that continue while the pump runs are different.

Persistent air at the returns is evidence that air is entering or remaining in a system intended mainly to move water.

## First decide whether the symptom is persistent

Record when the bubbles occur.

Do they:

- stop after startup;
- continue steadily;
- become worse at a certain pump speed;
- appear only when a suction cleaner is connected;
- appear only with one valve configuration;
- occur together with weak return flow;
- occur together with lower filter pressure;
- appear while the pump basket carries a large air pocket?

A symptom that follows one operating configuration can isolate the problem faster than a general inspection.

## Check the pool water level

A low water level can allow the skimmer to pull air.

Watch the skimmer while the pump is operating.

If it repeatedly forms a vortex or draws air, correct the water-supply issue before expecting the pump to maintain a stable prime.

Also confirm that the skimmer basket and weir are not creating an obvious restriction.

## Observe the pump basket

On many systems, the pump basket can be observed through a clear lid without opening anything.

Compare it with the pool's normal appearance.

Useful clues include:

- a large persistent air pocket;
- surging between water and air;
- a basket that repeatedly loses water;
- difficulty maintaining prime.

This becomes more important when return flow is weak or pressure is lower than normal.

## Inspect owner-visible suction points

Depending on the installation, normal owner checks can include:

- suction-cleaner hose connections;
- visible hose damage;
- accessible unions;
- pump-lid condition;
- visible suction plumbing;
- valve positions or stems.

Do not randomly tighten, dismantle, or modify unfamiliar components.

The purpose of these checks is to identify whether one visible branch introduces air.

## Use configuration as a test

Suppose the pool is bubble-free during normal skimmer operation but bubbles begin every time a suction cleaner is connected.

That change is evidence.

Inspect the cleaner hose and its normal connections before dismantling the pump or filter, because the symptom follows that branch.

If bubbles continue regardless of cleaner use, broaden the investigation to water level, skimmer behavior, pump-lid condition, valve state, and other owner-visible suction clues.

## Read air with pressure and flow

Air becomes more useful when combined with the other measurements.

Examples:

- **low pressure + weak flow + air** points toward inadequate water reaching the pump or suction-side air;
- **normal pressure + weaker flow + air** makes air entry or configuration worth investigating;
- **high pressure + weak flow with little air** points more strongly toward filter or downstream resistance.

Use the [filter-pressure guide](/pool-troubleshooting/pool-filter-pressure-high-or-low/) to read these combinations against the pool's clean baseline.

## Repeated loss of prime is a stop signal

A pump that repeatedly fills with air, cannot maintain a water supply, or runs with little or no water is more than a cosmetic bubble problem.

Do not leave it running indefinitely in the hope that it will recover.

If the pump repeatedly loses prime, develops unusual heat or noise, or cannot establish normal circulation after owner-safe checks, shut it down and move to equipment-specific service as appropriate.

## Verify the repair

The issue is resolved when:

- persistent air no longer reaches the returns;
- the pump maintains prime;
- circulation returns to the established baseline.

If the original symptom also included cloudy water, recheck the water condition after circulation is stable. A circulation problem can contribute to poor filtration and clarity, so the visible water problem may not fully recover until normal water movement is restored.
