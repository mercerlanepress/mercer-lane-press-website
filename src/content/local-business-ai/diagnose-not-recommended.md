“Why are competitors appearing and not us?” is useful only after it becomes a more specific diagnostic question.

## Check the failure category

Work through these areas in order:

1. **Identity:** Are core facts contradictory across important sources?
2. **Relevance:** Do your pages state the exact services, customers, and locations involved in the test?
3. **Proof:** Is there legitimate evidence a customer can evaluate?
4. **Corroboration:** Do reputable third-party sources independently connect the business with those facts?
5. **Access and freshness:** Are the important pages public, current, and technically retrievable?

A weakness in one category can look like a mysterious “AI SEO” problem when it is really an ordinary evidence gap.

## Compare sources, not just competitors

When another business appears, note which sources the answer used. A competitor may be surfaced from its own website, a business profile, a review platform, an industry directory, or a local article. That source pattern is often more informative than the order of the names.

Do not copy a competitor's claims. Ask instead what legitimate evidence they have that you do not.

## Fix factual errors before visibility

An incorrect recommendation can be worse than no recommendation. If answers show an old phone number, wrong service area, outdated brand name, or unsupported credential, trace the likely source and correct it where you legitimately can.

## Retest under controlled conditions

Use the same small set of realistic service-and-location prompts after meaningful changes. Avoid drawing conclusions from a single answer because wording, location, freshness, and available sources can change outputs.

Track what changed, what did not, and which sources recur. Then use the [measurement guide](/local-business-ai/measure-ai-visibility/) to keep the results defensible.
