HR work routinely involves information that should not be copied casually into a public AI tool.

A safer starting question is: **what is the minimum context this draft needs?**

## Use placeholders where possible
For practice and drafting, labels such as “Employee A,” “Marketing Coordinator,” or “Manager B” can often provide enough context without exposing a person's identity.

Avoid combining a real person's identity with sensitive performance, salary, health, disciplinary, complaint, or other confidential information unless your organization has explicitly approved the tool and the use complies with its privacy and information-security requirements.

## Separate the drafting job from the confidential record
Many tasks need the shape of the situation, not the complete case file. A difficult-conversation draft may need the observed behavior, expected standard, and desired next step. It usually does not need every historic note about the employee.

## Treat output as a draft
AI can produce inaccurate, inappropriate, or overconfident wording. Verify facts against the source material, check tone, and apply the organization's own policy and review process.

Policies, contracts, disciplinary communications, compliance documents, accommodations, investigations, union-related material, and other higher-risk work may require qualified legal or compliance review before use.

The practical principle is simple: **minimize input, review output, and use approved systems for sensitive work.**
