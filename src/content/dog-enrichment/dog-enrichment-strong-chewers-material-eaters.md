Cheap DIY dog enrichment is not automatically the safest choice for a strong chewer or a dog that swallows materials.

*Dog Enrichment for Apartments & Busy Owners* makes the rule simple: **if the dog turns the prop itself into something to eat, change the prop category**.

## Stop repeating the same ingestion risk

Cardboard tubes, boxes, towels, fleece mats, plastic cups, soft containers, and packing paper can all become swallowing hazards for the wrong dog.

More intense supervision does not make an unsuitable material suitable.

If a dog repeatedly tears and ingests cardboard, the answer is not to build a more complicated cardboard puzzle. Switch to an activity with fewer chewable parts.

## Use open searches on checked surfaces

A clean floor or rigid tray can provide foraging without giving the dog a destroyable container.

Options include:

- **Kibble Constellation** on a checked non-slip floor;
- **Kibble in a Baking Tray** using a metal tray;
- **Slow Scatter Breakfast** in a clean confined area;
- a short visible food search using only the meal and the room.

For dogs that vacuum up non-food debris from the floor, use an appropriate rigid feeder or another controlled setup instead.

## Prefer prop-light social and training games

Some of the most useful options need almost no equipment.

**Hand Target Circuit** uses the owner's hand. **Owner Hide-and-Find** uses the owner as the search target. **Find Me Around the Corner** uses a safe doorway. **Slow Back-Up** can be taught on clear non-slip flooring without a loose object.

These activities can avoid the exact material categories a dog tends to shred.

## Use known safe toys rather than improvised packaging

A **Toy Hide** can work for a dog that carries a durable familiar toy safely.

Keep the hiding place easy and accessible. Do not put the toy inside cardboard, fabric, or another chewable package merely to make the game harder.

For durable food toys or rigid puzzles, choose equipment appropriate to the dog's size and chewing style, inspect it before use, and replace it when damaged.

"Strong chewer" labels do not remove the need for inspection and supervision.

## Fabric activities are conditional

The book includes towel, blanket, snuffle-mat, and fabric-related ideas only with clear conditions.

If the dog eats fabric, skip them.

A dog that nudges and searches through a towel safely may use **Towel Roll Dinner** or **Blanket Island Search**. A dog that tears and swallows cloth should use a different category entirely.

The same principle applies to resting mats and soft toys.

## Cardboard destruction is not a default activity

The manuscript includes **Cardboard Destruction Box** only for dogs that reliably spit cardboard out, with tape, labels, staples, and plastic removed and close supervision throughout.

At the first sign of ingestion, the activity stops.

For a material eater, use the open-search version or skip the box entirely.

## Chewing itself needs suitable materials

**Chew Station Rotation** uses a size-appropriate chew or durable chew toy selected for the individual dog's chewing style, dental health, and diet.

The book warns against using excessive hardness as the route to longer duration. It specifically advises avoiding very hard items such as antlers or hooves where tooth damage is a concern.

If a chew becomes small enough to swallow, damaged, cracked, or otherwise unsafe, it should be removed.

## Keep a no-prop backup menu

A useful strong-chewer menu might include:

1. hand targets;
2. owner hide-and-find;
3. a checked floor or metal-tray search;
4. route choice through safe rooms;
5. a durable familiar toy search;
6. quiet observation or another activity that does not put ingestible material on the floor.
