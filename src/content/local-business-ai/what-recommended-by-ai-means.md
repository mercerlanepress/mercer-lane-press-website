A local business can appear in several different ways in an AI-assisted answer. It may be **mentioned** by name, **cited** as a source, described accurately without being recommended, or presented as one of several possible providers. Those are different outcomes, so do not collapse them into a single “AI ranking.”

## Start with four jobs

Before a system can plausibly surface a business, there generally needs to be enough public evidence for four jobs:

1. **Discovery.** The business or relevant sources need to be accessible.
2. **Identification.** The system needs to recognize one business rather than several conflicting versions.
3. **Fit.** The available material needs to explain what the business does, for whom, and where.
4. **Corroboration.** There should be enough legitimate evidence to support the description.

That leads to a useful owner diagnostic: identity, relevance, proof, corroboration, and access/freshness. It is not a published platform formula. It is a way to organize work you can actually inspect.

## Why ordinary local search still matters

AI-assisted answers do not make ordinary search foundations obsolete. Clear service pages, accurate business profiles, consistent contact details, crawlable pages, and legitimate third-party references are still useful because they create retrievable evidence.

Avoid tactics that exist only to sound “AI optimized.” If a change would make the business less clear to a customer, it is unlikely to be a good foundation.

## Measure the result you actually got

When you test a prompt, record more than “yes/no.” Note which businesses were surfaced, what sources were cited, whether your facts were accurate, and whether the answer showed uncertainty. Repeating the same small prompt set over time is more useful than chasing a mysterious universal score.
