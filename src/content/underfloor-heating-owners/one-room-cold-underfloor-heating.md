**When one room is cold and nearby rooms are comfortable, the cause is not automatically a blocked pipe or failed actuator.** Controls, floor coverings, room heat loss, sensor location and a zone's actual design can all produce similar symptoms.

## Room, zone and loop mean different things

A **room** is the space you occupy. A **zone** is an area with separate heating demand. A **loop** is a water circuit under the floor. One zone can operate several loops, and one thermostat can serve more than one space. Old manifold labels may no longer match a renovated property.

This matters because turning a guessed loop valve to see which room cools down can disturb system balancing and give misleading results in a slow-response floor. Leave active loop mapping and balancing to a competent professional.

## Compare four facts across the rooms

| Question | A useful observation |
| --- | --- |
| Are the targets and modes really the same? | Record current air temperature, target, mode and time for each relevant thermostat. |
| Does one room actually fall behind? | Compare measured room-air shortfalls, not whether tiles feel warmer than carpet. |
| Are the rooms physically comparable? | Note glazing, drafts, exposed walls, insulation, sunlight, door use and different floor finishes. |
| Has something recently changed? | Note carpets, rugs, underlay, thermostat settings, building changes and weather conditions. |

A room with more glazing may struggle in cold, windy conditions while an internal hall remains comfortable. The pattern is important; it does not establish a proven cause.

## A practical observation record

Suppose the lounge reads 18°C against a 20°C target, while the kitchen has reached 20°C. First check whether the lounge is in its normal comfort period and the thermostat is showing a **measured air temperature** rather than a floor sensor. Record the demand indicator's meaning from the model manual.

Then note whether the problem repeats at similar times, whether the weather was unusually cold and whether floor coverings changed. An isolated reading after a deep setback is weaker evidence than a recurring shortfall during otherwise normal operation.

Use a simple record:

- Room, date and time
- Measured air temperature and target
- Thermostat make/model, current mode and confirmed meaning of any heat symbol
- Other rooms' corresponding temperatures and conditions
- Recent changes, accessible fault messages and how long the problem has persisted

## What a specialist might investigate

A qualified system professional may examine correct zone mapping, room heat loss, circulation, controls, sensor position, commissioned output and heat-source behaviour. These are **professional checks**, not a troubleshooting sequence for an owner to imitate.

Ask the specialist which findings are confirmed, what the proposed remedy will change and how performance will be checked afterwards. Request an updated zone map if the existing handover documents are incomplete.

## When to stop the comparison

A leak near electrical equipment, repeated protective trip, dangerous floor temperature, gas or carbon monoxide concern or other immediate hazard takes priority over data gathering. A persistent seriously cold room also needs timely professional attention, especially when occupants are vulnerable.

If **all** rooms are cold, start instead with the heat source's ordinary mode and user-visible errors, not a single zone diagnosis.
