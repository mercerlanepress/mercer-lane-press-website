Another dog can be more valuable than food, especially for a highly social dog. That does not mean recall is impossible. It means social access needs to become part of a controlled proofing plan.

The first job is to find a distance where your dog can notice the other dog and still respond.

## Start outside the lock-on zone

Do not begin with the other dog at greeting distance.

Use a helper dog where practical, or work around naturally occurring dogs from generous distance without interfering with them. Your dog should be able to notice the other dog, eat, move, and orient back toward you.

If the dog freezes, strains, vocalizes, or cannot look away, increase distance before using the recall cue.

A recall given after attention has completely locked on is often a test rather than a useful training repetition.

## Use notice, reconnect, recall

A simple three-step loop makes the setup clearer:

1. Let the dog **notice** the other dog.
2. Reinforce a voluntary **reconnection** or create enough distance for one to happen.
3. Give one **recall** cue at short, achievable range and reward the complete return.

If step two is not available, do not force step three. Increase distance.

Record the distance from the other dog, not just the distance of your recall. That is often the variable that determines whether the dog can succeed.

## Make helpers boring during the recall

A cooperative person with a calm helper dog can make practice much easier to control.

Start far away with the helper stationary. During your recall, the helper should remain neutral instead of calling, moving excitedly, or offering attention.

Only change one feature at a time:

- the helper moves;
- the helper comes a little closer;
- the recall starts from farther away;
- your dog works after a brief greeting;
- play lasts slightly longer before the exit.

Changing several of those at once makes it difficult to know what caused a failure.

## Sometimes let recall lead back to the social opportunity

If an appropriate greeting is planned and both dogs are comfortable, a powerful pattern is:

**notice → recall → reward → release to greet**

That teaches that checking in does not always cancel social access.

The same principle can work during suitable play. Call during a natural pause, reward close to your body, briefly touch or secure the harness, and often release back to play.

Do not wait for chasing or wrestling to reach maximum intensity before asking for the first exit.

## Train leaving play as a separate skill

A dog may recall well before greeting but fail once play begins. Treat that as a different stage.

Start with a very short, low-arousal interaction. Recall during a pause, reward, handle briefly, then release back when appropriate. Increase the duration of play only while exits stay clean.

The goal is to build many successful departures, not to let play escalate until the dog is unable to respond and then repeatedly call.

## Do not use uncontrolled dog parks as the first laboratory

A crowded dog park gives you little control over distance, movement, greeting style, or the behavior of other dogs.

If recall around dogs is weak, cleaner setups can include:

- a known helper dog;
- parallel walking at distance;
- a secure private field;
- a controlled class or training area;
- the edge of a public area with generous space.

A dog park may later provide opportunities for easy recalls if the dog is already reliable in secure group play, but it is a difficult place to build the skill from scratch.

## Distinguish social excitement from fear or reactivity

A dog that wants to greet presents a different problem from a dog that is barking, lunging, panicking, snapping, or showing intense fear.

Recall can be useful as an exit behavior, but it is not a treatment plan for aggression or fear. If distress or safety risk is involved, prioritize distance and management and consider veterinary or appropriately qualified behavior support.

The [Reactive Dog Walking hub](/reactive-dog-walking/) covers walking-specific threshold and exit planning.

For ordinary social proofing, keep the standard recall rules: one cue, complete arrival into handling reach, a worthwhile reward, and a simpler setup after two failed or assisted repetitions.
