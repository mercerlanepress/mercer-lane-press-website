The 2026–2030 Technician Class exam is easier to plan for when you understand the blueprint rather than treating the official pool as one long list.

A Technician exam contains **35 questions**. The official blueprint draws **one question from each of 35 groups** in the pool.

The current pool covered by this guide contains **409 questions** in total.

That means the study job is not simply “memorize 409 answers.” A more useful question is:

**Can I handle an unfamiliar question from every group that can appear on the exam?**

## The ten subelements

The 35 questions are distributed across ten subelements:

| Subelement | Exam questions | Pool questions | Main area |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| T1 | 6 | 68 | Rules |
| T2 | 3 | 37 | Operating procedures |
| T3 | 3 | 35 | Propagation |
| T4 | 2 | 23 | Amateur radio practices |
| T5 | 4 | 50 | Electrical principles |
| T6 | 4 | 46 | Components |
| T7 | 4 | 44 | Practical circuits |
| T8 | 4 | 47 | Signals and emissions |
| T9 | 2 | 23 | Antennas and feed lines |
| T0 | 3 | 36 | Safety |
| **Total** | **35** | **409** | **One question from each of 35 groups** |

Rules carry the largest single share, but no group can simply be ignored because every group contributes one question to a normal exam form.

## Use the group code as your diagnostic label

When you miss a practice question, write down the **group code**, not only the question number.

For example, a miss in T5D tells you the weakness is in the Ohm's Law, series, and parallel group. A miss in T7C points you back toward antenna and feed-line measurements and troubleshooting.

That creates a more useful review loop:

1. identify the group;
2. review the concept behind the miss;
3. explain why the tempting wrong answer is wrong;
4. try a fresh question from the same group;
5. check whether the same weakness reappears in a mixed mock.

This is more informative than repeatedly retaking one fixed practice test.

## A four-step learning loop for each group

A practical group-by-group study unit can follow four steps:

1. **Read the big picture.** Understand what decisions or distinctions the group is testing.
2. **Work the high-yield concepts.** Learn the facts, relationships, formulas, or operating ideas that make the answers make sense.
3. **Study the traps and scenario.** Separate similar terms, numbers, components, or operating situations.
4. **Complete a short drill without looking back.** Retrieval is the test of whether the idea is available when the wording changes.

After all 35 groups have been studied, mixed mock exams become a way to find what still needs work rather than a substitute for learning.

## What score is a pass?

A passing score on the real 35-question Technician examination is **26 correct answers**.

The study method in *Ham Radio Technician Exam Prep 2026–2030* uses a higher readiness target: aim for **three consecutive mixed mock scores of 30/35 or better**, with no recurring weakness in safety or rules.

That 30/35 target is a **study recommendation from the book**, not an FCC requirement and not a guarantee of passing.
