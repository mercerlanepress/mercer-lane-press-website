A safe word is useful only as one part of a plan. It may help challenge an impersonator, but a word can be overheard, shared, or discovered. **The stronger rule is to end an unexpected request and call back using a number the family already trusts.**

## Build the plan together

1. **Choose the people included.** Include children, older relatives, caregivers, and anyone who might receive an urgent request on the family's behalf.
2. **Choose a memorable phrase.** It should not be a pet name, birthday, address, school, or fact visible on social media. Do not store it under an obvious label such as “family safe word.”
3. **Set the callback rule.** Agree that any unexpected request for money, secrecy, login help, or a security code ends before verification. Each person uses a saved number or a second trusted relative.
4. **Set payment boundaries.** Decide that no urgent transfer, gift card, cryptocurrency purchase, cash collection, or new payee is approved from an incoming call or message alone.
5. **Name backup contacts.** Write down at least two people to contact if the person supposedly in trouble cannot be reached.
6. **Practice without fear.** Run a short example: “I need money now and cannot explain.” The receiver ends the conversation, calls back independently, and checks with a backup contact.
7. **Review the plan.** Update phone numbers and replace the phrase if it has been exposed. Revisit the plan after changes in caregiving, travel, or family contacts.

## What the safe word can and cannot do

A correct word increases confidence but should not authorize a payment by itself. A wrong or missing word is a reason to stop, not a reason to bargain with the caller. Voice recognition is also insufficient: audio can be distorted, replayed, or imitated, and stressful situations make people more likely to hear what they expect.

## Make refusal easy

Give every family member permission to hang up and verify, even if the caller sounds upset or authoritative. A genuine relative can tolerate a safety check. Agree on a neutral phrase such as, “I always verify urgent requests; I will call you back.”

Print the trusted contacts for relatives who do not use a smartphone, but keep the sheet in a private place. For a live event, use the [urgent-message verification steps](/ai-scam-defence/verify-urgent-family-message/) rather than relying on the safe word alone.
