A strong local service page has two audiences but one standard: **clarity**. A customer wants to know whether the business can solve the problem. Retrieval systems benefit from the same explicit subject, readable text, coherent headings, factual detail, and evidence.

## Start with the customer's decision

Before writing, answer:

- What exact service is this page about?
- Who is it for?
- Which locations are genuinely served?
- What problem or decision brings the customer here?
- What is included and excluded?
- What does the process look like?
- Which facts, policies, examples, qualifications, or reviews legitimately support the claims?
- What should the customer do next?

A generic article about why a service is important often avoids these buying questions.

## Make fit extractable without sounding robotic

A useful page usually makes it possible to understand a simple statement: the business provides a particular service for a particular kind of customer in a defined area, using a process and evidence the customer can evaluate.

Use descriptive headings and visible text. Define abbreviations. Put the direct answer close to the question. Use tables only when they genuinely make comparison easier.

## Publish unique evidence

Generic service copy is easy to generate. More useful material comes from the real operating details only the business can publish honestly: job types declined, preparation requirements, common customer misunderstandings, access constraints, process stages, policies, and what changes the scope of work.

Do not invent expertise to fill a template.

## Keep structured data aligned with visible content

Machine-readable markup should reflect what the page actually says. Do not use structured data to make claims that are absent from the visible page.

If pages are clear but the business still does not appear where expected, use the [diagnostic guide](/local-business-ai/diagnose-not-recommended/) rather than immediately creating more content.
