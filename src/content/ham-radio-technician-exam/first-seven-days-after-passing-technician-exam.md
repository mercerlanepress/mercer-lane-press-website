Passing the Technician exam gives you a natural next question:

**What should I do first?**

The study guide closes with a deliberately small seven-day sequence. The aim is to turn exam knowledge into basic operating familiarity without trying to learn every part of amateur radio in the first week.

## Day 1: Verify the FCC grant before transmitting

Passing the examination is not itself permission to transmit.

Wait until the operator/station grant appears in the FCC license database.

This is the same licensing principle tested in the rules section of the Technician pool: the database grant is the operative record.

## Day 2: Program one local repeater correctly

Use one repeater as a practical setup exercise.

Work through the items that appear repeatedly in Technician study:

- receive frequency;
- transmit offset;
- access tone or code where required;
- squelch;
- the difference between repeater output and input.

Do not try to program every repeater in the region at once. One correct example teaches more than a long unverified memory list.

## Day 3: Make a short local contact

Keep the first contact simple.

Listen first, identify properly, and use the ordinary operating habits you studied.

The point is not to produce a perfect performance. It is to connect the exam's rules and procedures to an actual exchange.

## Day 4: Try 146.520 MHz simplex

The Technician material uses **146.520 MHz** as the national 2-meter FM simplex calling frequency.

A simplex contact helps make the distinction between repeater operation and transmitting and receiving on the same frequency concrete.

Remember that real operation still depends on current privileges, local conditions, band plans, and good operating practice.

## Day 5: Check the antenna system

Turn the antenna and feed-line chapters into a real station check.

The study material connects antenna work with ideas such as:

- resonance;
- SWR;
- connectors and feed-line condition;
- antenna analyzers;
- dummy loads for transmitter testing;
- the effect of antenna height on VHF/UHF range.

Use the appropriate tools and safe procedures for your equipment rather than improvising around electrical, RF, tower, or power-line hazards.

## Day 6: Join a net as a listener first

Listening to a directed net makes several exam concepts visible at once:

- net control;
- checking in;
- formal traffic;
- phonetics;
- disciplined channel use.

Listening before participating gives you a chance to hear the local procedure.

## Day 7: Choose one next skill

The book suggests choosing one next direction rather than trying to do everything.

Examples include:

- satellite receive;
- APRS;
- emergency communication;
- a simple antenna project;
- starting the General Class syllabus.

One next skill gives the first week somewhere to lead.

## Keep the exam material as a reference, not as operating authority

The Technician study guide is concept-focused and exam-oriented.

For real operating decisions, use current FCC rules, current official information, local procedures, equipment documentation, and any appropriate safety or professional guidance.

For the exam-frequency anchors most likely to recur in study, see the [Technician privilege landmarks guide](/ham-radio-technician-exam/technician-license-privilege-landmarks/).
