A green pool often triggers one immediate thought: **algae**.

Algae may be a strong possibility, especially when the water is both green and cloudy and sanitation has not been maintained. But color alone should not decide the treatment.

A more useful first question is:

**What kind of green am I actually seeing?**

## Separate the appearance into three starting patterns

### Green and cloudy

Biological growth becomes more plausible when green coloration is accompanied by cloudiness, slippery or brushable growth, low sanitizer, or a recent period when normal sanitation, circulation, or filtration was interrupted.

That does not mean every green-and-cloudy pool has exactly the same cause.

Ask what allowed the condition to develop:

- Did sanitizer demand rise after heavy swimmer use, storms, debris, heat, or sunlight?
- Was the pump off or running differently?
- Has sanitizer-generation equipment changed status?
- Is the filter loading rapidly?
- Is circulation weaker than normal?
- Is the stabilizer/CYA context relevant?
- Could the test result itself be unreliable?

The useful diagnosis is not simply **algae**. It is **why the pool stopped maintaining the conditions that normally prevent it**.

### Green but unusually clear

Clear green water deserves a different pause.

Recent chemical additions, source-water changes, dissolved metals, or developing staining can contribute to unusual coloration.

Record what changed before the color appeared. If the water became green shortly after a product was added or after a significant refill, do not automatically pile on another treatment.

When you cannot confidently distinguish biological growth from metal-related discoloration, reliable water analysis or professional assessment is more useful than stacking corrective products.

### Green material mainly on surfaces

Look at steps, shaded areas, ladders, corners, and locations with weaker water movement.

Brush a small area. If the material lifts easily, that supports a biological explanation, but it still does not replace water testing and a circulation/filtration check.

Recurring growth in the same physical location can also point toward a repeated circulation or cleaning weakness.

## Check the pool as four connected systems

Use the book's four-system model:

1. **Water condition and chemistry**
2. **Circulation**
3. **Filtration**
4. **Equipment and environmental load**

For a green-water problem, useful checks can include:

- current free chlorine;
- pH;
- stabilizer/CYA status when relevant;
- filter pressure compared with the clean baseline;
- return-flow strength;
- pump and sanitizer-system operation;
- recent swimmer load, weather, debris, or chemical additions.

Do not add every available test simply because the pool is green. Test because the result will help separate plausible causes.

## Use the timeline

A pool that slowly turned green during several hot days with declining sanitizer is a different diagnostic problem from a pool that turned clear green shortly after a chemical addition.

Write down:

- the last time the pool looked normal;
- the first time the color changed;
- what was added;
- whether the pump or filter was serviced;
- whether a storm occurred;
- whether the pool was used unusually heavily;
- whether water was added or removed.

The timeline often narrows the problem before another product is opened.

## If algae is well supported

Once active biological growth is the best-supported explanation, treatment normally involves restoring appropriate sanitizer conditions while keeping circulation and filtration effective.

The exact product choice, concentration, quantity, application method, protective equipment, waiting period, and retesting instructions come from the **current label of the product actually being used**.

Do not transfer a dose from one chlorine product to another merely because both are sold for pool sanitation.

Do not mix chlorine products, and do not mix chlorine with acid.

If circulation is inadequate or the filter is heavily loaded, chemistry alone may not complete the recovery.

## Verify more than color

A greener-to-bluer change is useful, but it is not the whole endpoint.

Verify that:

- normal clarity returns;
- sanitizer can be maintained;
- return flow remains normal;
- filter pressure behaves as expected;
- surface growth does not quickly reappear.

If the water clears temporarily but the same problem returns, use the [chlorine-loss guide](/pool-troubleshooting/chlorine-keeps-disappearing/) and treat recurrence as new evidence rather than simply repeating the previous treatment harder.

If pressure rises and flow weakens while the pool is clearing, use the [filter-pressure guide](/pool-troubleshooting/pool-filter-pressure-high-or-low/) to decide whether filtration has become the next limiting step.
