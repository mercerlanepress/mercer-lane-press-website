The final day is not the time to reread an entire exam-prep book.

The final-day review in *EPA 608 Universal Certification Exam Prep 2027* compresses the material into a short set of anchors, then moves to exam logistics.

## Ten final review anchors

1. **Universal = Core + Type I + Type II + Type III.** The book notes that a proctored Core is required for Universal.
2. **Phaseout milestones:** January 1, 1996 for most U.S. CFC/Class I ODS production/import; January 1, 2020 for the virgin R-22 production/import servicing milestone described in the book.
3. **Small appliance:** specified factory-made, factory-charged, hermetically sealed product with five pounds or less of refrigerant.
4. **Type I recovery:** 80% if the compressor is dead or equipment is pre-1993; 90% with post-1993 equipment plus a working compressor; or the 4 in Hg vacuum alternative.
5. **Type II evacuation:** high pressure under 200 lb = 0/0; high pressure 200+ lb = 4/10; medium pressure under 200 lb = 4/10; medium pressure 200+ lb = 4/15; very high pressure = 0/0.
6. **Low pressure:** 25 mm Hg absolute in both equipment eras.
7. **Current covered ODS leak triggers described in the book:** industrial process refrigeration 30%, commercial refrigeration 20%, comfort cooling 10%, other 10%.
8. **Repair timing:** normal 30 days; 120 days when an industrial process shutdown is required.
9. **Cylinder and pressure-test safety:** do not fill a recovery cylinder beyond 80%; use nitrogen rather than oxygen or compressed air for pressure testing where allowed.
10. **Type III sequence anchors:** excessive purging can suggest air infiltration; recover vapor after liquid; heat oil to 130 F before removal; circulate or remove water during deep evacuation; recharge vapor first.

If one of those anchors feels vague, return to the full rule and condition rather than memorizing the isolated number.

## Check all four mock sections separately

The book uses a readiness target of at least **21/25 in each section on both full mocks**.

Do not rely only on an overall total.

If one section is below the target, spend final review time on that section's specific misses.

For each miss, write what created the confusion:

- the number;
- the date;
- the pressure unit;
- the appliance class;
- the rule condition;
- a current versus legacy value.

That list is a better final review sheet than random rereading.

## Provider logistics to confirm

The book's “Before You Book or Sit the Exam” checklist says to confirm directly with the organization administering your exam:

- that the provider is currently EPA-approved for the certification path you intend to take;
- whether your Universal exam is proctored;
- how identification is checked;
- any remote-proctoring equipment or room rules;
- the number of questions;
- the time allowed;
- the retest procedure;
- fees;
- the score required by that provider for each section;
- whether any Core result you plan to use is acceptable for Universal.

The book specifically warns that an open-book Core cannot be used to obtain Universal.

## Recheck current information

The edition states that its regulatory facts were researched and fact-checked on **September 10, 2026**.

It also states that rules, guidance, test banks, and provider procedures can change.

Before the exam, recheck current EPA rule pages and any provider correction sheet for changed:

- dates;
- thresholds;
- pressure values;
- legacy wording;
- exam logistics.

## What not to do on the final day

Avoid adding a large amount of new material.

The 21-day plan in the book ends with light final review, exam logistics, sleep, and no new material.

Use the final day to make the information you already learned easier to retrieve, not to rebuild the course from the beginning.
