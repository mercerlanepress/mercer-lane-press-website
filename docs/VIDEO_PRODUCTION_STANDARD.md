# Mercer Lane Press — premium promotional video standard
**Established:** 3 November 2026  
**Baseline:** The approved November 2026 Mercer Lane Press premium flat-vector editorial motion-graphics style. Preserve its strengths; do not restart as a generic slideshow.

## Creative brief
Start with one specific homeowner, contractor, reader or genealogy problem that the *actual* published book or product addresses. A typical vertical short is about 27–35 seconds (use other lengths when the brief requires), 9:16 with clear mobile-safe typography. Story arc: intriguing reader problem → original visual explanation/example → useful insight → genuine book/product reveal → direct verified CTA. The viewer should learn something even if they never buy.

## Visual direction
- Create original coherent SVG/vector illustrations with subject-specific visual detail (cutaway house, changing heat-flow diagram, contractor cost breakdown, family-record comparison, product UI).
- Animate what the explanation *means*: relationships, decisions, comparisons, causality, movement and transformations. Use connected shape morphs, camera movement or masking only when they improve comprehension.
- Use a consistent brand hierarchy, deliberate whitespace, restrained colour and typography. Follow approved exemplars and any current actual brand assets; do not invent a universal palette.
- Make diagrams and captions readable on a phone; keep important text away from platform overlays and crop areas.
- Use authentic final approved cover and real product screens. Never fabricate product capabilities, testimonials, results, or book pages.
- Retain editable project files, vector sources, script, scene plan, licensed/generated-media provenance and final render when possible.

## Explicit prohibited defaults
- **No whizzing/whooshing/swooshing sounds at scene transitions.**
- No arbitrary bounces, meaningless spins, generic moving icons, text-only slide decks, or one-effect-for-every-scene.
- No automatic full-screen flashes, gratuitous transitions, cramped small type, unlicensed audio or misleading mockups.
- No repeated phrases, exaggerated claims, unsupported sales claims or invented Amazon/website URLs.
- Avoid voiceovers when they add little; if used, check pacing, pronunciation, intelligibility and source rights.

## Audio
Original restrained instrumental music should support the story, not dominate it. Prefer quiet clean transitions and subtle, selectively justified accents instead of continuous sound effects. Check sound-on playback for abrupt joins, clipping, music/voice balance and unwanted whizzes. Synthetic sounds may be generated locally where technically and legally suitable, but locally generated does **not** automatically mean free of hardware, licensing or time costs.

## Production pipeline
1. **Source audit.** Read relevant published content and verify CTA URL. Capture approved cover or screenshot.
2. **Director brief.** Specify audience, problem, emotional progression, teaching point, desired visual grammar, music mood and explicit bans.
3. **Storyboard.** Record for each shot: seconds, message, visual objects, animation purpose, on-screen copy, sound and transition logic.
4. **Build.** Create an editable vector/HTML animation project and render to a real MP4. Test a segment if evaluating a new renderer such as HyperFrames; do not change tools purely for novelty.
5. **Contact-sheet critique.** Sample representative frames from *every* shot, including beginning/end states and scene boundaries. Inspect typography, composition, continuity, source accuracy and crop.
6. **Listen and review motion.** Watch the entire MP4 with audio. Static thumbnails alone cannot reveal bad movement, transitions, timing or unwanted sound effects.
7. **Rank defects.** Score story, subject accuracy, animation meaning, design, mobile readability, audio, CTA and pacing on a consistent 1–10 working scale. Identify the three highest-impact issues; scores are subjective diagnostics, not guarantees.
8. **Revise and rerender.** Fix the weak elements and repeat the contact-sheet + full playback check. Block delivery if a critical error persists.
9. **Delivery.** Provide a verified MP4, editable sources when practical, known audio/media provenance, title/caption/CTA text and any platform-specific variants requested.

## Scene continuity and reusable sources
For a coherent animated explanation, establish a **visual continuity lock** before final rendering: core subject geometry, view/camera, background, permitted colours, correct real product assets, and text-safe zones. Give each scene a concrete start/end visual state; where appropriate, reuse the previous ending state as the next scene's starting state. A transition should explain the next causal step, not merely hide a cut.

For generated-image or time-lapse material, preserve the approved reference frame and check each successive stage for subject drift, impossible physical transformations and visual discontinuity. Label illustrative or simulated visuals clearly where a viewer could mistake them for actual site footage or tested product output.

Use a scene table with seconds, exact on-screen text, purpose, source evidence, start/end state, animation method, audio and purchase destination. Build reusable, editable assets from the approved treatments and retain the director brief and test results alongside the MP4. Prefer a small pilot of a new open-source animation framework, with verified licence/security/quality, over changing the entire toolchain for novelty.

## Quality gate
A release candidate passes only if:
- Text remains legible on a phone and does not cover the book or primary illustration.
- Every major motion supports explanation, narrative or visual continuity.
- There are no whizzing transition sounds, clipping, abrupt silence, incorrect labels or off-sync beats.
- The real book/product and supported customer benefit are shown, with the precise approved CTA URL.
- The final render has actually been played/inspected; no unverified claim of passing QA.
- Any untested element is disclosed in the final handoff.

## Reuse and experiments
Keep successful shot structures and reusable vector components, but vary meaningful hooks and examples rather than mass-producing near-identical ads. If testing variations, compare retention, relevant link visits and verified sales over matched periods; view counts alone do not prove commercial success. One approved reference video is a creative baseline, not a mandate to copy protected third-party material.
