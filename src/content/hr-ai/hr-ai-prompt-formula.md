Useful HR prompts do not need elaborate wording. They need enough structure to stop the model guessing.

The book's core formula is **Role + Task + Context + Output**.

## Role
State the working perspective needed for the draft: for example, an HR business partner, recruiter, HR coordinator, people analytics specialist, or internal communications specialist.

## Task
Name one concrete job. “Help with recruiting” is broad. “Create eight behavioral interview questions for a project-manager role” gives the model a defined task.

## Context
Add only the facts needed to make the draft specific: role, department, company size, issue, timeline, audience, or tone. Do not add sensitive employee data merely because it is available.

## Output
Say what the usable result should look like: a short email, numbered questions, a table, talking points, a structured document, or a concise executive summary.

## Diagnose weak output
If the result is too generic, add relevant context. If the format is wrong, tighten the output instruction. If the tone or level is wrong, make the role more specific. If the response drifts off task, sharpen the task.

The aim is a useful **first draft**, not an automatic final HR decision or document. Check facts, tone, company policy, and any legal or compliance implications before use.
